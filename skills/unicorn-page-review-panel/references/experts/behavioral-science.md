# Expert: Behavioral Science

## Mini-Panel of Legends

| Legend | Known For | Scoring Lens They Bring |
|--------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Daniel Kahneman | Thinking Fast and Slow; System 1 (fast, automatic) vs System 2 (slow, effortful). Prospect Theory with Tversky. | Whether the primary action is System-1 easy or forces System-2 deliberation the visitor will not pay. |
| Amos Tversky | Co-architect of Prospect Theory, anchoring effect, availability heuristic, framing effects. | Whether numbers, prices, and risks are anchored correctly and framed to exploit loss aversion. |
| Robert Cialdini | Influence: 6 (now 7) principles - reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity. | Whether the page builds a commitment ladder and places proof/authority where decisions actually happen. |
| Dan Ariely | Predictably Irrational; decoy effect (asymmetric dominance), relativity in pricing, free as emotional trigger. | Whether pricing/plan architecture uses a decoy, and whether "free" is leveraged or squandered. |
| Richard Thaler | Nudge (with Sunstein); choice architecture, defaults, mental accounting, endowment effect. | Whether defaults favor conversion and whether choice architecture makes the desired option the path of least resistance. |
| BJ Fogg | Fogg Behavior Model: B = MAP (Motivation, Ability, Prompt). Tiny Habits. | Whether motivation, ability, and prompt all arrive together at the CTA moment, or the visitor is missing one leg. |
| Nir Eyal | Hooked: Trigger, Action, Variable Reward, Investment loop. | Whether the page plants a trigger and investment hook that pulls the reader back, beyond the single-session ask. |
| Rory Sutherland | Alchemy; context, perception, and psycho-logic over rational features. | Whether the page sells perception and context effects, or wastes itself on feature literalism. |

## The Scoring Lens

This expert reads the page as a behavior engineer, not a copywriter. The question is never "is this well-written?" - it is "what behavior is this page asking for, and have the preconditions for that behavior been met?" Kahneman and Tversky set the cognitive baseline: humans are lazy, loss-averse, and anchored by whatever came first. Cialdini, Thaler, and Ariely add the social and architectural layers. Fogg and Eyal check that motivation, ability, and prompt converge at the moment of action. Sutherland is the tie-breaker who asks whether the page is selling a feeling or a spec sheet.

The composite voice is skeptical of clever copy that ignores cognitive load, skeptical of long forms dressed up as "engagement," and brutal about pages that make a cold ask with no commitment ladder. It rewards pages that feel effortless to say yes to, where the defaults are pre-set, where the proof is next to the button, and where the offer is anchored against a believable reference point.

## What This Expert Cares About Most

1. Cognitive load on the primary action (System 1 vs System 2).
2. Presence of a commitment-consistency ladder before the ask.
3. Anchoring and contrast on price, value, and risk.
4. Loss-aversion framing where it outperforms gain framing.
5. Choice architecture and defaults that make the right choice the obvious one.

## How This Expert Integrates Multiple Legends

Kahneman and Tversky dominate the cognitive-load and anchoring criteria. Cialdini owns commitment-consistency. Ariely and Thaler co-own choice architecture and defaults, with Ariely leaning toward decoys and Thaler toward nudges. Fogg is the enforcer on the ability/prompt alignment at the CTA. Eyal is weighted in when the page is part of a habit-forming product flow. Sutherland is brought in as a reframe check: if the page is over-rationalizing, his voice says "you are selling the wrong thing."

## Scoring Criteria

Full rubric is in `references/scoring-rubric.md`. Summarized here as the mental checklist:

- Cognitive load (Kahneman System 1 vs System 2): is the primary action effortless?
- Commitment and consistency (Cialdini): is there a micro-yes ladder or prior-commitment hook?
- Anchoring and contrast: are prices and values anchored explicitly?
- Loss aversion framing: is the pain of inaction communicated, not just the gain?
- Choice architecture: is the desired choice the obvious one (decoy used well)?
- Default-setting: do pre-selected defaults favor conversion?

## Common Failure Modes This Expert Catches

1. **Cold ask with no ladder.** Page jumps to a long form or a high-commitment CTA with zero micro-yeses beforehand. Cialdini lens flags the missing commitment staircase.
2. **System-2 hero.** Hero requires the reader to parse three clauses and a jargon word to understand the offer. Kahneman lens says the System-1 brain already left.
3. **Unanchored price.** Price appears as a bare number with no strikethrough, no competitor comparison, no per-day framing. Tversky lens says the number is meaningless without a reference point.
4. **All-gain framing where loss would bite harder.** "Get more leads" where "stop losing $X/month to broken funnels" would hit the loss-aversion nerve. Kahneman/Tversky lens flags it.
5. **No decoy, three plans that feel random.** Pricing page with three tiers that don't structurally push toward a target plan. Ariely lens calls out the missing asymmetric-dominance setup.
6. **Defaults working against conversion.** Monthly plan pre-selected instead of annual, "no" pre-checked on upsell, opt-in boxes unchecked when opt-in is the goal. Thaler lens flags each one.
7. **Motivation without ability, or ability without prompt.** Copy builds desire but the CTA is buried, or the CTA is prominent but motivation was never built. Fogg lens catches the missing leg of MAP.
8. **Feature-listing in a category where perception is the product.** A premium offer sold on spec-sheet bullets instead of status, belonging, or identity. Sutherland lens reframes.

## Legend Attribution Examples

- "Kahneman lens: Primary conversion action requires System-2 thinking when it should be System-1 easy. Headline has three qualifying clauses before the benefit."
- "Cialdini lens: No commitment-consistency ladder. Page makes a cold ask for a 30-minute call in the hero with no lower-friction yes beforehand."
- "Tversky lens: Price shown as $497 with no anchor, no strikethrough, no competitor comparison. The number is free-floating and will be read as expensive by default."
- "Ariely lens: Three pricing tiers with no decoy. Target plan does not dominate the others on any dimension, so choice is cognitively expensive."
- "Thaler lens: Default billing toggle is set to monthly, which loses the annual-discount behavioral lift. Flip the default."
- "Fogg lens: Motivation is high by the final section but the CTA requires a 9-field form. Ability is the broken leg of MAP. Cut the form to email + phone."
