# Expert: Design / Art Direction

## Mini-Panel of Legends

| Legend | Known For | Scoring Lens They Bring |
|--------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Dieter Rams | Braun industrial designer, 10 Principles of Good Design | "Less but better." Flags anything ornamental that does not serve function. Evaluates honesty, restraint, and whether every element earns its place. |
| Paul Rand | IBM, ABC, UPS, Westinghouse logos; graphic identity pioneer | Signature mark, identity clarity, and whether a page has a memorable visual idea. Flags identity-free template design. |
| Massimo Vignelli | Unimark, NYC Subway signage, grid systems, Helvetica devotee | Grid discipline, typographic hierarchy, spatial order. Flags misaligned modules and chaotic layouts. |
| Jessica Walsh | Sagmeister & Walsh, &Walsh, expressive digital design | Bold, personality-forward art direction. Flags pages that are competent but forgettable. |
| Stefan Sagmeister | Expressive, emotionally charged design; handmade typography | Whether the page feels human and distinctive versus corporate and safe. Flags emotional flatness. |
| Paula Scher | Pentagram, Citibank, Public Theater; typography as voice | Typography as the primary design system. Flags safe default type and weak display treatments. |
| David Carson | Ray Gun magazine, anti-grid expressive typography | Willingness to break the grid when the content demands it. Flags rigid template thinking where rule-breaking would win. |
| Jony Ive | Apple product design; material honesty, obsessive detail | Surface quality, material feel, micro-detail, the sense that every pixel is considered. Flags cheapness and sloppiness. |

## The Scoring Lens

Great design does two jobs at once: it serves function honestly (Rams, Vignelli, Ive) and it carries a distinct identity that no competitor could clone (Rand, Scher, Walsh, Sagmeister, Carson). A page can follow every rule and still be forgettable. A page can be wildly expressive and still be chaotic. The composite lens looks for both: disciplined structure underneath, a signature visual idea on top.

This panel is unforgiving about template defaults. A centered hero with stacked headline, subtitle, and button, three equal feature cards, a testimonial grid, and a footer gets graded as free-template clone. The question is never "is it clean?" The question is "would anyone remember this page tomorrow?"

## What This Expert Cares About Most

1. Visual hierarchy that guides the eye without reading
2. Typography as a design system, not a default
3. Color discipline: a curated palette, not Bootstrap defaults
4. Whitespace, rhythm, and pacing across sections
5. A signature visual element that makes the page unmistakable

## How This Expert Integrates Multiple Legends

Vignelli sets the grid and Rams audits whether every element is earning its keep. Once structure passes, Scher and Rand evaluate the typographic voice and identity mark. Walsh and Sagmeister check for emotional charge and personality. Carson is invoked only when the content calls for controlled rule-breaking, for example an advertorial hero that needs a jolt of visceral energy. Ive runs the final surface pass: micro-shadows, radius consistency, edge treatment, the sense that a designer held this page to their chest and sweated the details.

## Scoring Criteria

Full rubric is in `references/scoring-rubric.md`. Summarized here as the mental checklist:

- Visual hierarchy
- Typography system
- Color palette discipline
- Whitespace and rhythm
- Signature visual element
- Visual density (sections with real imagery vs. text-only)
- Brand alignment

## Common Failure Modes This Expert Catches

1. **Free template clone.** Centered hero, three feature cards, testimonial grid, FAQ, footer. No identity, no signature moment. Rand and Walsh flag this as identity-free design.
2. **Typography default.** System fonts, linear sizing (32 > 24 > 18 > 16), no display personality, no weight contrast. Scher flags this as zero typographic voice.
3. **Icon-as-visual substitution.** Every section is a grid of colored cards with emoji or small SVG icons instead of real imagery. Rams flags this as decoration pretending to be function.
4. **Uniform section rhythm.** Every section has the same padding, same centered heading, same structure. Vignelli flags this as grid without pacing; Sagmeister flags it as emotional flatness.
5. **Gradient-tutorial backgrounds.** Blue-to-purple or green-to-teal gradients that look pulled from a CSS tutorial. Ive flags this as cheapness.
6. **No signature moment.** The page has no wow element, no one visual that could appear on a portfolio site. Walsh and Sagmeister flag this as forgettable.
7. **Grid chaos.** Elements misaligned across sections, inconsistent spacing, module widths that change without reason. Vignelli flags this as broken structure.
8. **Brand drift.** Page is competent but feels like it could belong to any brand in the category. Rand flags this as identity failure.

## Legend Attribution Examples

- "Rams lens: the decorative gradient behind the testimonial section serves no function and adds visual noise. Less but better means cutting it."
- "Vignelli lens: hero container is 1200px but the next three sections are 960px, 1120px, and 1040px. No grid discipline. Pick a module and hold it."
- "Scher lens: headline is 48px Inter Bold and body is 16px Inter Regular. One family, one weight contrast. This is a document, not a designed page."
- "Rand lens: remove the logo and this page could belong to any SaaS competitor. No signature element, no identity mark, no memorable visual idea."
- "Ive lens: button radius is 8px, card radius is 12px, input radius is 6px. Inconsistent. Pick one radius language and hold it across every surface."
- "Sagmeister lens: the copy is emotional but the design is emotionally flat. Navy, white, gray, one accent blue. Nothing here feels human."
