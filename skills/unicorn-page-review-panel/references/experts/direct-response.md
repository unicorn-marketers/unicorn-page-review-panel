## The Scoring Lens

This expert reads a page as a single-purpose selling machine. Every element either moves the reader toward the offer or is dead weight. Kennedy's rule applies: if the page is trying to look nice, it is not trying to sell. Beauty for its own sake is a cost, not a benefit, unless it specifically increases belief, desire, or urgency.

The lens weights offer, risk reversal, and proof stacking above creative or brand tone. A page with an ugly design and a surgically clear offer beats a page with beautiful design and a fuzzy offer every time. The expert treats the page the way Hormozi treats a VSL: perceived value must obviously exceed price, risk must be reversed, and the reader must feel stupid for saying no.

## What This Expert Cares About Most

1. **Offer clarity.** Can the reader state the offer in one sentence within 10 seconds of landing? Kennedy's test.
2. **Risk reversal.** Specific, named, bold guarantee. Not "satisfaction guaranteed." Something like "60-day full refund, keep the bonuses." Hormozi stack standard.
3. **Proof stacking.** Multiple proof types within reach of every CTA. Brunson's rule: new opportunity needs new proof.
4. **CTA rhythm.** CTAs appear at natural decision points, not only far below the fold. Kennedy would add three more.
5. **Urgency honesty.** If urgency is used, it is real. Fake countdown timers get a hard flag. Kern and Kennedy agree: fake urgency kills long-term list economics.
6. **Close strength.** The final CTA restates stakes, repeats the guarantee, and makes the reader feel the cost of inaction. Clemens and Goff lens.

## How This Expert Integrates Multiple Legends

On offer design and clarity, Kennedy and Hormozi dominate. On funnel logic and the hook-story-offer sequence, Brunson leads. On big idea and unique mechanism, Todd Brown is the final word. On the lead and belief-shifting VSL architecture, Goff and Clemens lead. On risk reversal, Hormozi's grand slam framework is the scoring anchor. On results-in-advance and story-driven pre-sell, Kern leads. On traffic-to-page fit and message-to-market match, Perry Marshall's 80/20 lens weighs in (most pages fail here because they were built without a specific traffic source in mind).

- Offer clarity (Kennedy, Hormozi)
- Urgency honesty (Kern, Kennedy)

- Close strength on the final CTA section (Clemens, Goff)

## Common Failure Modes This Expert Catches

1. **Fuzzy offer.** Reader cannot state what is being sold in one sentence after 30 seconds. Kennedy would scrap the page and start with the offer.
2. **Fake urgency.** Countdown timer that resets. Hormozi and Kern both flag as a trust killer with long-term traffic economics.
3. **Weak or missing guarantee.** "Satisfaction guaranteed" without terms, length, or named refund policy. Hormozi lens: risk is not actually reversed.
4. **Single proof type.** Three testimonials and nothing else. No stats, no logo row, no press. Brown lens: proof stack is one-layer deep, belief will not hold.
5. **One CTA far below fold.** Reader who is ready to buy in the first screen cannot act. Kennedy would add CTAs at every natural decision point.
6. **Feature dump in the lead.** Page opens with feature bullets before establishing pain, mechanism, or big idea. Brown and Clemens lens: belief shift sequence broken.
7. **Traffic-to-page mismatch.** Cold traffic landing on a product detail page. Perry Marshall 80/20 lens: wrong message for the list.
8. **Weak close.** Final section trails off. No restatement of stakes, no guarantee repeat, no urgency. Goff lens: closes are written last and show it.
