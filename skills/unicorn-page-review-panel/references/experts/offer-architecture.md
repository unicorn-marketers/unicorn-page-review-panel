## The Scoring Lens

This expert reviews the offer itself, not the copy around it. The question is not "is the page persuasive" but "is the underlying offer constructed so that saying no is harder than saying yes." A brilliantly written page wrapped around a weak offer scores low. A plainly written page wrapped around a Grand Slam offer scores high.

The composite philosophy: value stack beats copy, anchor beats price, guarantee beats claim, scarcity must be honest or banned. Hormozi owns whether the offer is stack-worthy. Poundstone, Benson, and Ariely own pricing presentation and choice architecture. Kennedy owns risk reversal. Cialdini owns scarcity integrity.

## What This Expert Cares About Most

1. Value stack clarity and itemized perceived value
2. Anchor price presence and credibility
3. Guarantee specificity and boldness
4. Decoy and plan structure on pricing pages
5. Full risk stack (reversal plus scarcity plus bonuses)

## How This Expert Integrates Multiple Legends

Hormozi scores value stack and overall offer strength. Poundstone and Benson co-score anchor presence and price framing. Ariely scores plan structure where multiple options exist. Kennedy scores guarantee design. Cialdini scores scarcity honesty and flags any fake urgency for an automatic sub-70. When Hormozi says the stack is weak and the page scores well on everything else, Hormozi wins the overall score because a weak offer cannot be rescued by presentation.

## Scoring Criteria

Full rubric is in `references/scoring-rubric.md`. Summarized here as the mental checklist:

- Value stack clarity
- Anchor price present
- Guarantee design
- Decoy / anchor plan design (for pricing pages)
- Payment plan framing
- Risk stack (reversal + scarcity + bonuses)

## Common Failure Modes This Expert Catches

1. **Floating price** - A number appears with no retail, no strikethrough, no comparison. Poundstone flags anchor absence.
2. **Generic guarantee** - "Satisfaction guaranteed" without terms, days, or mechanism. Kennedy flags. The guarantee should be bolder than the objection.
3. **Value stack as bullet list** - Features listed without perceived dollar values, no total, no contrast to price. Hormozi flags.
4. **Fake countdown timer** - Timer resets on page reload, or counts down to a date that will simply reset next week. Cialdini flags and forces an automatic failure on urgency honesty.
5. **Three-plan grid with no anchor** - Good/Better/Best with identical visual weight and no decoy. Ariely flags missing asymmetric dominance.
6. **Bonus dump** - Bonuses listed but no perceived value, no reason they exist, no scarcity on the bonuses. Hormozi and Kennedy co-flag.
7. **Payment framing buried** - "$97/month" when the annual price broken to per-day would be $3.23. Benson flags framing miss.
8. **Risk-forward offer** - Buyer pays full price up front, guarantee is 7 days, refund requires a form. Kennedy flags risk stack failure.

## Legend Attribution Examples

- "Hormozi lens: the stack lists six deliverables with no perceived values and no total. Stack them with dollar anchors and make the total 5x the price."
- "Poundstone lens: the price $497 floats with no anchor. Add a strikethrough retail of $1,997 and the decision architecture changes."
- "Ariely lens: the three plans are visually identical. Restructure the middle plan as the target and the left plan as the decoy with fewer features at a nearly identical price."
- "Kennedy lens: the guarantee reads 'we stand behind our work.' Replace with '60-day no-questions refund, keep the bonuses.'"
- "Cialdini lens: the scarcity is a countdown timer. If the deadline is not real, remove it. Fake scarcity kills trust worse than no scarcity."
- "Benson lens: $1,200 annual presented as $1,200 annual. Reframe as $3.28 per day, less than a coffee."
